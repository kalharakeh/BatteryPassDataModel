FigureAttributesWhy a reader caresRS
Capacity and voltage#57, #66, #64, #65How much the battery holds and at what voltage: the first thing anyone comparing batteries checks.✓✓
Power#67, #70What it can deliver, at high and low charge (the old card's "Original power capability").✓✓
Expected lifetime#82, #85, #19How long it should last, and the warranty that backs it.✓✓
Round-trip efficiency#71, #72How much energy comes back out, new and at half its life.✓
Carbon footprint#30, #35, #31–#34The regulation's headline environmental figure and its class; the stages show where it comes from.✓✓
Recycled and renewable content#49–#52, #53What share of the key metals is recycled (the old card's donuts), and the renewable share.✓✓
Chemistry#41, #42What is inside, for anyone handling, buying or recycling it.✓
Storage and fire#88, #89, #26Safe idle temperatures, and what to put out a fire with: first responders and warehouses.✓
Two smaller choices, with a default. (1) Power is stored in W (171,000 W); the card shows kW for reading, the sections keep W. (2) The carbon pie becomes one bar, which fits a tile and reads on a phone; the pie can stay if the owner prefers it.

Not shown, on purpose: battery status (#22), state of charge (#63), cycles so far (#83), temperature (#87) and the fades are purchaser data, never public; the documents and symbols are links, already in their sections.
